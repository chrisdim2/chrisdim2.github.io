Privacy Policy

App: Apollo · Last updated: October 4, 2026

In short: Apollo has no accounts, no usage analytics, and no ads, and we keep no data about you. Your photos stay where they already are, in your own cloud account, and your trips and boarding passes stay on your device. We run two small helper services purely for technical reasons (Box sign-in and Wallet passes), and they store nothing (see section 2).

2. What we collect: nothing that we keep

You don't create an account with us, you don't give us an email address, and the app contains no analytics, crash-reporting, or advertising SDKs. We don't track you across apps or websites, and we don't use the IDFA. No photo or video ever passes through our systems.

There are exactly two small helper services, run by us on Cloudflare. Each one exists because there's something that can't be done safely from inside the app:

3. What Apollo is

Apollo is a viewer for photos you already keep in your own cloud: pCloud, Dropbox, OneDrive, Box, WebDAV, any S3-compatible service, or your device's Photos library. The app connects directly from your device to the service you chose. You can also transfer photos directly between your own accounts (see section 6).

It also keeps track of your trips: flights, ferry journeys, and their boarding passes, which you enter yourself or scan from your tickets.

5. Permissions the app requests

Each permission is requested only when you first use the feature that needs it, and all of them are optional.

  • Photos: for two things. First, to read and display your device's Photos library if you connect it as a source. Second, to save photos you download or transfer from a cloud account into it.
  • Camera: to scan the barcode on a boarding pass so the flight details fill in automatically. Recognition happens on the device, and no image is stored or sent.
  • Calendar: write-only access, to add a trip to your calendar when you ask. The app can't read your calendar.
  • Notifications: reminders before a departure. They are scheduled on the device, and nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Background processing: so syncing, EXIF reading and smart analysis can continue after you leave the app.
  • Siri and Shortcuts: if you use them (e.g. “Search photos in Apollo”), your voice is processed by Apple under its own terms. The app only receives the text you asked for and opens the matching screen.

The app doesn't ask for location access. The locations you see on the map come from your own files (EXIF GPS), not from where you are. You can revoke any permission at any time in the Settings app.

7. Connecting your cloud accounts

Sign-in uses OAuth, the standard that lets an app get access without ever seeing your password. You enter your password directly with the provider. The app receives only an access token, stored in your device's Keychain, which you can revoke at any time:

  • in the app, under Settings → the provider → Sign Out,
  • or in the provider's own security settings.

Box is the one exception. Box requires a secret for sign-in that can't be stored inside an app, so that one step goes through our helper service (section 2), which forwards it without keeping anything. For WebDAV and S3-compatible services, you enter the credentials yourself, and they're stored in the Keychain.

Each provider's privacy policy applies: pCloud, Apple, Dropbox, Microsoft (OneDrive), Box, or the policy of your own WebDAV/S3-compatible provider.

8. Family mode

If you turn it on, the app writes two small files to the photo folder of the cloud account you choose as the family hub (pCloud, unless you pick another of your connected accounts), so that everyone with access to that folder can read them:

  • .photoapp-family.json: album covers, year groupings, place names, the member names you set up, your trips (name, dates, who went, transportation, expenses, and ratings), your flights and ferry journeys, your boarding passes (passenger name, seat, booking code, and barcode), and the list of shared photos moved to the Trash.
  • .photoapp-exif.json: capture dates, GPS coordinates, and camera models, so each device doesn't have to read them again.

Boarding passes are shared on purpose: one person scans everyone's tickets, and each member finds theirs on their own phone.

These files contain location data, people's names, and boarding passes. Anyone with access to the shared folder can read them, and a booking code with a barcode can be enough to view or change a booking. "Members" are just labels you type in yourself, not accounts, and there's no sign-in. Turn this feature on only with people you already trust with your photos and your tickets. You can turn it off, or share only part of it (without trips, for example), in Settings → Family mode. The files are stored in your own cloud account, not with us, and you can delete them at any time.

The "I am" setting (which member you are on this device) and your choice of family hub are not synced; they stay on the device.

9. How long data is kept

We don't keep any data, so there's nothing to retain: the helper services keep nothing after responding. On your device, data is kept for as long as you want. You can clear thumbnails and temporary files in Settings, turn off smart analysis (which also deletes its results), delete trips and passes, sign out of a provider (which also removes its entries from the index), or delete the app, which removes everything. Your photos in the cloud aren't affected.

10. Support

If you contact us from Help → Contact, the message is pre-filled with technical details that help us diagnose the problem: app version, OS version, device model, which clouds are connected, whether Family mode is on, the number of photos in the index, free space, and language. No file names, locations, photos, trips, or credentials are included. You can see these details before sending and delete them. Support emails are kept only as long as needed to resolve the issue.
