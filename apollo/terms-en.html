1. What the app is

Apollo is a viewer and organizer for photos and videos you already keep in your own cloud (pCloud, Dropbox, OneDrive, Box, WebDAV, or any S3-compatible service) or in your device's Photos library. By reading your files' metadata, it shows a timeline, albums by year, a location map, and search. It isn't a storage service, and it doesn't replace your backups.

The app modifies your files. When you correct a date or location and choose to write it to the files, crop a photo or apply filters, trim or merge videos, or transfer photos from one of your cloud accounts to another, the file at the destination is replaced or created. Deleting something in the app first moves it to the Trash (Settings → Trash), where you can restore it. Files are permanently deleted from the cloud either automatically, after the retention period you set there (or never, if you chose "Never"), or manually from the Trash screen. At that point, deletion is permanent and can't be undone. Back up your files before making bulk changes.

3. Dates and locations

The app tries to find the real capture date from each file's EXIF data, file name, and year folder. When those are missing or wrong, the date shown is an estimate. The same applies to the "Estimate Location" feature, which borrows coordinates from photos taken around the same time. Estimates are marked separately in the app, and you can confirm or undo them. Don't rely on them as evidence.

The same goes for smart analysis: what a photo shows, which one is “best”, and which photos are identical or similar are automatic estimates made by Apple’s tools and can be wrong. Duplicate photos are never deleted without your confirmation, and they go to the app’s trash first, where you can restore them. Check your selection before you confirm.

4. Family mode

If you turn it on, the app writes settings files to the shared folder of the cloud account you choose as the family hub. These files include your photos' capture dates and GPS coordinates, the member names and trips you've set up, and your boarding passes, including booking codes and barcodes. Anyone with access to that folder can read them. It's your responsibility to turn this feature on only for folders you share with people you trust. See also the Privacy Policy, section 8.
