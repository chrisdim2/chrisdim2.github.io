1. About Iris

Iris is a macOS app that gets your photos and videos ready before they go into your library: it fixes capture dates and locations, adds titles, descriptions, and keywords, renames files in bulk with sequential numbering, and finds duplicates. It isn’t a storage service and doesn’t replace your backups.

Iris changes your files. When you click Save, dates, locations, titles, descriptions, and keywords are written into the files themselves. For videos, this means repackaging the file (a remux) without re-encoding it. Renaming changes file names on disk. Iris first writes to a temporary file, checks that it opens, keeps a backup copy where needed, and records the previous state so you can undo. These steps reduce the risk but don’t eliminate it: undo can’t restore files you’ve changed or moved outside the app in the meantime. Always back up your files before making bulk changes.

2. File formats

RAW files (such as NEF, CR2, and ARW) can be read but not edited — Iris explicitly blocks saving them. We can’t guarantee that every format, codec, or unusual file will be read or saved correctly. The two parts of a Live Photo are linked by an internal identifier, which Iris reads but never changes.

3. Dates and locations

When you get a date from a file name, shift dates, or set the same date on many files, the result is what you asked for — not a verified record of when the photo was actually taken. The same goes for coordinates you pick on the map or enter by hand. Iris flags dates that look wrong (such as the year 0021) as suspicious, but the final call is yours. Don’t rely on metadata as evidence.
