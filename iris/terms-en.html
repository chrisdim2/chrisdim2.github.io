1. What the app is

Iris is a macOS app that prepares photos and videos before they go into your library: it fixes the capture date and location, writes title, description and keywords, renames in bulk with sequential numbering and finds duplicates. It is not a storage service and does not replace your backups.

The app modifies your files. When you press Save, the date, location, title, description and keywords are written into the files themselves. For videos the write is a re-package (remux) without re-encoding. Renaming changes file names on disk. The app first writes a temporary file, checks that it opens, keeps a rescue copy where needed and records the previous state so you can undo. These reduce the risk, they don't eliminate it: undo cannot restore files that you changed or moved outside the app in the meantime. Keep backups before making bulk changes.

2. File formats

RAW files (e.g. NEF, CR2, ARW) are read but not written: the app blocks the write explicitly. We don't guarantee that every format, codec or unusual file will be read or written correctly. The two halves of a Live Photo are tied together by an internal identifier that the app reads but never changes.

3. Dates and locations

When you recover a date from a file name, or apply an offset or a common date to many files, the result is what you asked for, not a verified real capture time. The same goes for coordinates you pick on the map or type by hand. The app flags "suspicious" dates that look wrong (e.g. year 0021), but the judgement is yours. Don't treat the metadata as evidence.
