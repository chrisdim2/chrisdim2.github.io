A phone photo isn't just a picture. It can contain the **coordinates** of where it was taken, the time and the phone model. Photograph something you're selling in your living room, send the file, and the other person may see where you live.

Many social networks strip this data on upload. Email, cloud links and many classified ad sites don't always.

## On iPhone

When you share a photo, tap **Options** at the top of the share sheet and turn off **Location**. It applies to that one share.

## On Mac, with Photos

When you choose **File → Export**, untick **location information**. The exported file has no location.

## With Iris: strip everything

**Clean for Privacy** in [Iris](/en/iris/) removes **all** metadata from photos and videos, not just the location: date, device, faces, everything. It keeps only what the image needs to look the same, namely orientation and colour.

- Works on many files at once, and on **videos**.
- After cleaning it re-reads the file, and if anything that should be gone is still there, it leaves the original untouched.
- It all happens locally on your Mac, nothing is uploaded.

## A small tip

The clean **can't be undone**, on purpose: undo would mean keeping a copy of exactly what you wanted removed. Run it on copies of your photos, not on the originals in your archive.
