It's Sunday evening, you're on holiday in Greece, and the pharmacy round the corner is closed. Greek pharmacies take turns staying open at night, at weekends and on holidays, but which one is it today? Maps show "Closed" almost everywhere, and the notice on the pharmacy door doesn't help if you're not standing in front of it.

Here are three ways, from fastest to slowest.

## 1. With an app that knows the rota

[Chronos](/en/chronos/) shows the pharmacies on duty near you, in real time, along with the hospitals on call. You see the opening hours, the distance and the phone number, and one tap opens directions in Maps.

## 2. On the local pharmacists' association website

Every prefecture in Greece has a pharmacists' association, and most of them publish the day's rota on their website. Search Safari for "εφημερεύοντα φαρμακεία" (on-duty pharmacies) followed by the name of the town.

- In Safari, tap the share button and choose **Add to Home Screen**. Next time, the page opens with one tap.
- Check the date on the page: some sites switch to the next day's list in the morning, others at midday.

## 3. By phone

## Why maps aren't enough

Apple Maps and Google Maps know each pharmacy's regular opening hours, not the duty rota, which changes every day. So a pharmacy on duty may show as closed, and vice versa.

## In an emergency

If it's urgent, don't look for a pharmacy: call **166** (ambulance) or **112**, the European emergency number, which also answers in English.
