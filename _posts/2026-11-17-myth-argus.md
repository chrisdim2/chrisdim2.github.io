Look closely at a peacock's tail: it is full of "eyes". Greek mythology has an explanation for that, and it is the story of Argus.

## Argus in Greek myth

Argus Panoptes ("the all-seeing") was a giant with many eyes, a hundred according to tradition. In Ovid's telling, when he slept only two of them closed at a time; the rest stayed open and kept watch.

Hera set him to guard Io, whom Zeus had turned into a heifer. Zeus sent Hermes to set her free. Hermes played his pipe and told Argus stories until all hundred eyes had closed, and then he killed him.

## The eyes on the peacock's tail

Hera did not let her faithful guard be forgotten. She took his eyes and set them in the tail of her own bird, the peacock. That, the myth says, is why the peacock has "eyes" on its feathers.

Argus lives on in the language too: in English, someone "Argus-eyed" is someone nothing gets past.
